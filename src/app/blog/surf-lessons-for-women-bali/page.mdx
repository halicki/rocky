  Start with the part that surprises people. In the water, almost nothing changes. The wave does not know who is on the board. If you want the physical side answered properly, we have a whole piece on whether <Link href="/blog/do-you-need-to-be-fit-to-surf-bali" className="text-accent-blue hover:underline">you need to be fit to surf</Link> — the short answer is no, and that applies here without an asterisk.

<p className="text-text-secondary leading-relaxed mb-4">

Women do not learn more slowly, do not need a smaller board "because they are lighter" when a bigger board would serve them better, and do not need a gentler version of the lesson. What I have noticed across two decades is a tendency in the opposite direction: women arrive having already talked themselves out of it once, and then progress faster than the men in the same session because they do not try to force the pop-up. Surfing punishes force and rewards patience.
