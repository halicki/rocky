### This reverses a decision 08-31 recorded explicitly, and the reversal is argued, not slipped in

The 08-31 sweep commit (`e8fd5a0`) states its exclusion in words:

> *"og:url deliberately left out of scope: it is not a Google canonical signal and including
> it would make the sweep two patterns."*

Two claims. **The second is falsified by today's execution**: a single `sed` expression —
`s#(https://surfingwithrocky\.com/[^"]*[^"/])"#\1/"#g` — fixed all four surfaces in one pass,
155 occurrences, and a line-by-line diff check confirmed **every changed line differs from its
original by exactly one added slash and nothing else** (156 lines compared, 0 mismatches). It
was never two patterns; it was one pattern that 08-31 only looked at through one surface.

**The first claim survives, and og:url is not why this ran.** og:url is genuinely not a Google
canonical signal. The reason to sweep is the two surfaces 08-31 never enumerated:
`BreadcrumbList.item` and `Article.url` are structured-data fields Google reads, and Google's
own structured-data guidance asks for the canonical page URL in them. Emitting a URL there
that the site itself 308-redirects is an inconsistency of the same family as the canonical
mismatch — and it sat on **every one of the 38 blog posts**. og:url came along because it
falls under the same one-line pattern and leaving it behind would have preserved the exact
split-spelling problem for no benefit.
